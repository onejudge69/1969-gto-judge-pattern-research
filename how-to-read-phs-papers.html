How to Read PHS Papers, Cowl Tags and Broadcast Sheets

A short guide. Field names are shown blank or as clearly marked example data. Transcribe exactly what is printed and write ? for anything you cannot read.

The PHS invoice / billing history

FieldWhat it tells you
VINThe car's full serial number. Check it matches the VIN tag.
Invoice numberA billing number, for example ZGA ______. It is not a body or build sequence.
Invoice / ship dateWhen the car was billed and shipped. A billing date, not the build date.
Dealer order number, DONThe order number, for example 000__. Early Judges often show low numbers.
Dealer / dealer codeThe selling dealer and its zone-dealer code.
Ship-toWhere the car was sent. A code ending in 991, 992, 993 or 995 rather than a dealer code is common on early Judges.
Option codesLook for 554 THE JUDGE.
Color / trimPaint and interior codes as printed.

The cowl and trim tag

Example data, not a real car: 01B / 69 24237 / FM 00000 / 257 / 72 72

FieldWhat it tells you
Build week, 01BMonth 01 = January, week B = second week.
Style, 69 24237Model year 69, GTO two-door hardtop.
Plant and body number, FM 00000Fisher plant prefix and body serial number.
Trim, 257Interior trim code; 257 and 258 were the first Judge trims.
Paint, 72 72Lower and upper paint; 72 = Carousel Red.

The broadcast sheet

FieldWhat it tells you
Order number boxOn cars compared so far this carries the same number as the DON on the PHS invoice.
Scheduling dateThe date the car was scheduled in the plant.
Model / trim / colorShould match the cowl tag.
Ship-to / charge-toDestination text, sometimes with a street address.
Option codesLook for WT1 or 554.

Tips

  • Photograph every page, uncropped and straight-on.
  • The same VIN readable on the invoice, broadcast sheet and original sale paper makes the strongest case.
  • If two papers disagree, record both exactly.
